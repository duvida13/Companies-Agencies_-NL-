# Working context and research learnings

Updated 11 October 2026. Use [the master company table](../directory/all-companies.md) and [the eight research methods](methodology.md). This page keeps practical knowledge that should improve future company searches; update it as we learn.

## What we are trying to find

Companies connected to rope access work in the Netherlands: specialist contractors, broader industrial employers, inspection/NDT firms, lifting/rigging and scaffolding businesses, offshore/wind providers, building maintenance companies and staffing agencies. Look for work that may use rope access even when the company does not call itself a rope access company or advertise a current vacancy.

Retain international providers, trainers, partners, small businesses and uncertain leads with factual notes. Luis decides suitability and removals; do not assign job-fit rankings or silently filter companies. Rotterdam-area addresses are useful for visits, but discovery covers the Netherlands.

## What has helped find more companies

- **Search beyond the business label.** Mennens performs rope work despite being known for lifting/equipment. Eurosafe and Ropetech show that training or equipment activity does not exclude project execution. Inspect services before deciding what a company does.
- **Follow trade and sector terms.** NDT, crane/derrick work, E&I, telecom masts, coating, fire protection, glazing, silo cleaning and green facades can uncover firms missed by general rope-access searches. Ginkel's abseiling gardener vacancy revealed a green-facade team.
- **Explore different regions and languages.** Dutch terms such as industrieel klimmen, abseiltechnieken and touwtechnieken supplement English rope access. Include rope acces/rope-acces spelling variants, ports and industrial areas. Search sites outside .nl.
- **Read deep pages.** Hanab's own rope team appeared in a news page; Multimetaal's use appeared in a project description. Homepages alone are insufficient. Check services, projects, careers, contact pages and useful PDFs.
- **Trace partners without assuming another crew.** Mundo/SHS, Lapwing/Rope Access Rotterdam and EEIS/CIS show that an advertised service can involve a partner. Keep distinct company names visible and record the delivery relationship.
- **Resolve branding without losing names.** Strong in Heights is Lars Domenie Hoogtewerken's website/branding. ATI's domains, Sky Lines/alpineskyline, OEG/Bluestream, FØN/C-Ventus and TEAM/QIS should not create accidental duplicate organizations. Keep aliases searchable in the table.
- **Use recruitment and company social pages as discovery evidence.** Rope access may be mentioned within another trade's role. Historic vacancies can reveal capability or an employer name. Company-authored material may support execution when the website cannot be read.
- **Accept supplied discoveries.** Luis supplied Mennens and Strong in Heights. Record that source accurately; do not present them as independent search discoveries. Use each discovery to expand the search vocabulary and sectors.

## Errors and uncertainties to handle better

- Search-engine route 1 repeatedly returned unrelated results in earlier passes; route 2 plus direct company-page reading was more productive in those passes. If results are poor, vary the query or source rather than repeat ineffective searches. This is observed tool behaviour, not proof that one engine is always better.
- Extraction failed or changed across attempts for Bilfinger, Access Climbing Solutions, BCB Cleaning, Aerial and others. Retry another primary page, company-authored post or authoritative listing. Preserve indexed-only evidence and failure notes; inaccessible does not mean irrelevant.
- Generic cleaning advice or repeated template text can mention abseiling without showing the company delivers it. Seek a dedicated service, named project or employer-authored role. Keep the candidate while the evidence is unresolved.
- A translated Dutch page does not prove a Dutch crew. SGS's localized content pointed to Singapore capability; MISTRAS and Deutsche Windtechnik have local/group evidence that must be distinguished from a verified Dutch rope team.
- A Dutch project is different from a Dutch office. Swire's project recruitment, international providers' market flags, city service-area pages and customer logos must not be converted into unverified headquarters, crew or hiring claims.
- Old vacancies, crawl dates and undated application pages do not guarantee a job is open. L1 mentioned in one role does not establish general L1 acceptance; retain trade, NDT, offshore and language requirements when stated.
- Separate IRATA technicians, working methods and company membership. Trainer-only membership does not establish contractor execution; nonmembership does not rule it out. Old certificates and membership snapshots are dated evidence, not current certification.
- Do not guess unnamed agency clients, LinkedIn profiles, addresses or emails. A parent/partner's LinkedIn is not the candidate's own profile. Label switchboards, recruiters, owners, business development and operations accurately.
- Keep ambiguous contacts and locations explicit. RanaWorks has conflicting email text/links; some sites have postcode/address inconsistencies; some business locations are postal or appointment-only.
- Setup and repeated file organization previously displaced discovery. Spend research effort on finding and checking companies. Keep one company/contact table so updates do not require synchronizing multiple views.

## Useful query patterns

Vary one sector or region at a time, then follow up on the named company:

- `Nederland "rope access" "diensten" bedrijven`
- `"industrieel klimmen" bedrijf [province or town]`
- `"abseiltechnieken" [sector or industrial area]`
- `"rope access" [hijsen / rigging / NDT / onderhoud / telecom / wind / gevelreiniging]`
- `"IRATA" [trade] vacature Nederland`
- `site:[company-domain] [rope access / abseilen / projecten / vacatures]`

Previously useful niche: `"IRATA" "hovenier" Veenendaal`. Do not treat a query that merely names a company as verified discovery evidence. Follow returned primary sources.
